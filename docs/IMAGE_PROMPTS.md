## CT detection — final generation prompt

Create a concrete explanatory research figure for a Japanese academic portfolio, landscape 3:2. NOT an anatomical engraving, not abstract concept art. A rigorous radiology/computer-vision paper figure on warm ivory, narrow forest-green captions and muted burnt-orange segmentation overlays. Two large, realistic-looking but entirely synthetic grayscale axial abdominal non-contrast CT slices side by side, same anatomical slice before and after processing. Correct conventional axial CT appearance: vertebral body posterior midline, surrounding abdominal soft tissue, kidneys, liver, pancreas anterior to major vessels; no exterior rendering of a pancreas. Left slice unannotated, right the same synthetic slice with a subtle translucent amber contour delineating the pancreas and one small thin red box around a hypothetical suspicious region within it. A small horizontal arrow between the panels and a compact neural-network processing block below, showing image input -> pancreas segmentation -> suspicious-region localization. High-quality sober scientific visualization, readable dark CT panels on neutral ivory, thin rules and small sans-serif typography, no dramatic light, no glowing organs, no clinician photo, no hologram, no glossy 3D, no decorative AI brain. Use only these short English labels accurately: 'Non-contrast CT', 'AI-assisted detection', 'Pancreas region', 'Candidate finding', and a small bottom note 'SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATION — NOT CLINICAL DATA'. No patient identifiers, no real patient data, no invented accuracy, confidence scores, or performance numbers, no vendor logos. Keep CT panels the dominant elements, generous but efficient whitespace. This should visually explain published research into locating suspected pancreatic cancer regions in non-contrast CT; it must not imply definitive cancer diagnosis or be represented as an actual output of the researcher's system.
